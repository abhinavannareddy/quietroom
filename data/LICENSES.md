# Data licenses

Only small files live in `data/`. Everything here is derived from one openly licensed corpus or
generated by the scripts in `python/`.

## Speech: LibriSpeech (CC BY 4.0)

`data/speech/*.wav`, `data/noise/babble.wav` and `data/regression/noisy_white_5db.wav` are
derived from utterances of **LibriSpeech** `dev-clean-2` (the "mini LibriSpeech" subset).

- Corpus: V. Panayotov, G. Chen, D. Povey, S. Khudanpur, "LibriSpeech: an ASR corpus based on
  public domain audio books", ICASSP 2015.
- Source: <https://www.openslr.org/31/>
- License: **Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International (CC BY 4.0)**,
  <https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/>. The underlying recordings are from
  LibriVox, which are in the public domain.
- Changes made: utterances were peak-normalised to 0.7, padded with 0.5 s of silence at the
  start and 0.3 s at the end, and stored as 16-bit PCM WAV. `babble.wav` overlaps six *other*
  utterances (all from speaker 3000), so it never contains the test sentences. The exact
  utterance IDs are listed in `clips.json`.

To rebuild them, download the archive from the link above, extract it, and run
`python python/make_test_clips.py --librispeech <extracted dir>`.

## Noise: generated, no license needed

White, pink and 50 Hz hum noise are synthesised at run time by `python/evaluate.py` from a fixed
random seed. Nothing is stored or downloaded. Babble is the file described above.

## Not used

The PRD suggests the DEMAND noise set as an option. It is not included: the full set is large,
and synthetic noise plus babble was enough to measure the pipeline. Adding it would mean
checking its license (CC BY 4.0 at the time of writing) and keeping only short clips.

## Reference labels

LibriSpeech has no speech/non-speech annotation. The VAD labels used in the evaluation are
derived from the clean clips (a 10 ms frame counts as speech if its energy is within 35 dB of
the loudest frame). They are not human annotations.
